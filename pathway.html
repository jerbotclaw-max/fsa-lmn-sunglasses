The one-sentence law: an expense is FSA/HSA-eligible when it is "medical care" under IRC §213(d) — diagnosing, curing, mitigating, treating, or preventing disease, or affecting a structure/function of the body. IRS Pub 502 interprets this for glasses: eyeglasses "needed for medical reasons" qualify. Everything below is applying that sentence to specific products.

Step 1 — Find your product on this tree

Rx glasses / prescription sunglasses (made to a person's prescription) → Eligible. No LMN. Pub 502 names them; every administrator list has them. Your only job: accept FSA cards (see Step 4).
OTC reading glasses, any fixed power (+0.50…+3.00+) → Eligible. The only non-Rx glasses on every eligibility list — correcting presbyopia is itself the medical function. FSA Store: "Both prescription and nonprescription reading glasses are eligible." This includes progressive/no-line multifocus readers — same corrective function (Walmart sells multifocus readers tagged FSA/HSA-eligible; FSA Store list has no line-style restriction). Tinted readers (sun-readers): same corrective-function argument — lead the SKU description with the correction, note the tint.
Plano (non-Rx) sunglasses → Not eligible by default. Blocking sun is "comfort/prevention," which Pub 502 doesn't pay for. The only door is an LMN certifying a diagnosed condition (photophobia, post-cataract, retinal disease, TBI…) → use the 12 templates. Administrator approval is discretionary — always call first. FSAFEDS states it verbatim: sunglasses "Require letter of Medical Necessity signed by your doctor, plus detailed receipt."
Accessories (cases, chains, cleaning) → generally not eligible; some administrators allow if billed as part of a covered eyewear purchase. Don't build a SKU strategy on these.

Step 2 — Product compliance (what the SKU needs to exist legally)

  • Device class: all eyeglasses and sunglasses are FDA Class I, 510(k)-exempt devices — corrective spectacles (21 CFR 886.5915), noncorrective/sunglasses (21 CFR 886.5900), and OTC readers. No product-level approval or "certification" exists to obtain. The responsible party (foreign OEM's US importer/agent or you) completes establishment registration + device listing in FDA FURLS — generic device type, not per-SKU.
  • Impact-resistant lenses (mandatory): 21 CFR 801.410 requires all eyeglasses/sunglasses sold in the US to use impact-resistant lenses and pass the drop-ball test — this is a labeling + testing rule that applies to every pair, reader or Rx. The OEM/lab runs the tests and retains batch records; get copies for your files.
  • Labeling: diopter marked on readers; UV claims substantiated (ANSI Z80.3 / ISO 12312-1 test reports); warnings per FDA labeling rules.
  • Standards (voluntary but expected): ANSI Z80.1 (finished prescription lenses), ANSI Z80.3 (non-Rx sunglasses), ISO 12312-1 (sunglass UV). Not legally mandatory — but they're what an administrator, retailer, or plaintiff's lawyer expects to see behind a UV400 claim.
  • No Rx required to sell pre-configured stock powers. An Rx only enters when lenses are made to an individual's prescription. Selling stock +0.50/+0.75 readers needs no optician, no exam, no sign-off — this is the CVS rack.

Step 3 — The FSA/HSA eligibility claim itself

  • There is no SKU certificate. Nobody "certifies" a product FSA-eligible. Eligibility is determined at claim time by the administrator applying §213(d)/Pub 502 to the receipt.
  • Getting paid by FSA cards at checkout requires merchant-side rails: IIAS/SIGIS certification (POS auto-approves eligible SKUs) or a payment wrapper (Flex, Truemed Payments, Sika Health) that runs eligibility/attestation and underwrites the FSA-card acceptance — their onboarding is the closest thing to a "certification" that exists, and it attaches to your checkout, not your product.
  • For plano sunglasses with LMN: sequence matters — evaluation and LMN before purchase (see provider kit for why the reverse order is enforcement bait).
  • Truth in marketing (FTC §5): never say "guaranteed eligible/approved." Say "FSA/HSA-eligible per Pub 502; final determination rests with your plan administrator."

Step 4 — Launch checklist

  1. OEM: confirm FDA establishment registration + device listing + 801.410 impact-test records + ANSI/ISO test reports (get copies).
  2. You: FDA importer of record OR resell under OEM's registration; register your own establishment if you import.
  3. SKU copy written to the corrective function (readers) or paired with LMN flow (plano tints).
  4. Checkout: IIAS/SIGIS or Flex/Truemed/Sika wrapper live.
  5. LMN letters linked at point of sale for tinted SKUs (templates + telehealth partner).
  6. Marketing language scrubbed of guarantee claims; administrator-disclaimer present.
